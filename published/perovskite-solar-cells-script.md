## Slide 5 — Key Challenges for Commercialization

_Presenter: — · bullets_

, we're not just talking about efficiency anymore, we're talking about durability. And here's what's really holding perovskite solar cells back from the commercial mainstream: moisture breaking down organic components, heat accelerating ion migration, and elevated temperatures increasing defect density. These aren't just technical hurdles – they're the fundamental reasons why these promising cells can't make it to market yet. Moisture penetrates through encapsulation layers and reacts with the perovskite lattice, triggering phase transitions that cause irreversible loss of photovoltaic performance. Heat degrades structure by accelerating ion migration within the crystal lattice, disrupting charge transport pathways. And elevated temperatures increase defect density in perovskite films, causing rapid degradation of charge carrier mobility over time. These five key challenges from our research are what prevent commercialization – weak material interactions, efficiency loss, temperature limitations, environmental degradation, and insufficient stability. This is why we're seeing performance drop to 20% of initial levels, and why the devices can't last more than a few years under real-world conditions. We've got to address these fundamental issues if we want perovskite cells to join the solar mainstream.

## Slide 9 — PSC vs. Si: Efficiency & Stability Gap

_Presenter: — · bullets_

Here's the stark reality that defines perovskite solar cells today: we've achieved efficiency levels matching commercial silicon technology, pushing 25.8 percent. But when it comes to stability, we're looking at a completely different world. While silicon cells can reliably operate for over 20 years, our perovskites degrade within months under normal conditions. This isn't just about performance—it's about practicality. The devices we're building today last less than 1,000 hours, while commercial silicon systems are designed to endure decades. And that's before we even get to the manufacturing challenges. Processing requires inert environments, because these materials are so sensitive to moisture and oxygen. We've made progress in structural evolution, moving from traditional architectures to inverted designs, from having both electron and hole transport layers to exploring HTL-free configurations. But despite all these advances, the fundamental issue remains: our perovskites still lack the long-term stability needed for commercial viability. The gap between what we can achieve in lab settings versus real-world applications is simply too wide to ignore.
